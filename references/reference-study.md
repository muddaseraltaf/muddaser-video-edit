# Add a sample without replacing the library
Classify by the user's intent, even when a reference arrives in source/. Mark its role in local intake state so it is not selected for editing later. Prefer samples/ for future references; preserve existing originals and paths. Filenames may misdescribe the actual content. Record hash, duration, frame rate, dimensions and a descriptive local ID. Transcribe locally when speech matters. Inspect the entire timeline with a sparse overview, the first ten seconds more densely, and uncertain transitions at enough frame density to understand the movement. Widely spaced stills cannot establish exact easing or cut timing.

Write a private study: approximate time ranges, narrated anchor, visual evidence, explanatory job, layout/type/movement behavior, and useful adaptation. Distinguish observed behavior from inferred implementation parameters. Full mixed-track peaks cannot identify exact SFX; direct listening and isolated assets are different evidence. Do not claim retention improvement or verify a sample's factual allegations from its editing quality.

For depth, map foreground occluders, subject parts, backing shapes, shadows and hinge points. Inspect both entry and exit: concealed content should disappear before its cover closes. Distinguish flat planes and parallax from views revealing new surfaces that require geometry or matching footage. Check native frame rate before interpreting stepped motion as a timing defect. Save sampled ranges and review limitations so unchanged footage need not be resampled.

Update benchmark-library.md only with distinct transferable patterns, preference changes and known failures; put construction guidance in the relevant implementation reference. Keep raw transcript, screenshots and detailed hashes in the private study, not the public skill. New samples are cumulative; conflicting styling stays an option unless the user explicitly changes a preference. Avoid adding a new universal rule after every isolated issue.
