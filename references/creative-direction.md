# Creative direction — canonical preferences

## Hook and retention
The opening is a production priority, not finishing decoration. Before building the rest, write what the viewer sees, understands and wants to know at 0–2, 2–5 and 5–10 seconds. These are planning windows, not mandatory cuts. Establish a striking subject/action or selective phrase, escalate its meaning, then deliver a reveal, mechanism, demonstration or evidence that earns continued attention. A generic title over an otherwise unchanged shot is insufficient for this user's default style.

Choose a relevant cutout, evolving diagram, guided screenshot, product detail or purposeful kinetic phrase. Preserve source meaning and expression. Review the opening for a clear topic, legible focal point, meaningful development and readable settlement. Rework decoration that explains nothing, text covering the face, or an arrival immediately followed by an exit. Do not force another sample's claims or montage template onto unrelated material. Retention is an aim, not a measured guarantee or a demand for constant busyness.

## Visual storytelling
Each insert needs a job: evidence, explanation, emphasis, demonstration or transition. A real screenshot can stay for many seconds while a crop/highlight guides reading. Diagrams should show a verb or state change with persistent objects. Repeated generic cards, unrelated icons and labels such as Create/Adapt/Learn are insufficient as the main visual treatment.

Use full speaker for expression/pivots, temporary half screen for simultaneous explanation, a small face crop when graphics need room, and full-screen evidence or animation when meaningful. Return clearly. Keep the face at useful scale and inspect intermediate crops. A circle is an option, not a compulsory layout.

Select a compatible family from [benchmark library](benchmark-library.md). For image layers and dimensional motion, use [cutouts and depth](cutouts-and-depth.md) to choose the assets and construction method. There is no quota for effects; a held, guided source may explain more than several decorative transitions.

## Motion
Major movement should accelerate and decelerate: slow–fast–slow. Starting ranges from accepted feedback: complete shrink/slide/replacement ~1.5–1.7s; vertical half-screen layout move ~1s; gentler float cycles ~2.5–3s. These are choices, not quotas. Short accents can be brisk while big spatial movement remains smooth.

Preferred substantial replacement: whole framed speaker shrinks → exits left → incoming visual enters from the left at smaller scale → expands. Reverse actor roles on return. Arrange overlap to avoid a large blank canvas. Preserve the crop inside the moving wrapper.

Use curved paths, deliberate staging, trailing labels/shadows and depth differences when useful. One main action leads attention; supporting motion responds. Include readable holds. Do not keep every element moving or add springs/bounce indiscriminately.

## Type and color
Use selective important words/phrases rather than continuous subtitles by default. Urdu uses shaped RTL script, English/numerals isolated when needed. Animate complete shaped words or phrases; never split Arabic letters. Use masks/highlight sweeps and occasional size escalation. Give glyph ascenders/descenders generous mask space.

Prefer light, neutral or content-derived surfaces. The user rejected permanent opaque black half screens. Authentic dark screens can retain their own colors inside a readable insert. Keep cream/orange, light blue/coral and other sample-derived styles as alternatives. New samples enrich the library rather than replacing it. One coherent type/palette/motion system per edit may mix compatible techniques.

## Sound and assets
Speech leads. Use restrained travel/contact/drawing/click cues tied to actions, not a beep for every word. Verify the actual mix; do not copy numeric gain settings or loudness merely because a sample is louder. A demonstration's original sound may deserve space.

Use real relevant screenshots/recordings with provenance when available. When narration makes a factual claim, prefer a primary-source page or artifact and qualify the on-screen wording if the source is narrower than the speech. Generated illustration is useful for a missing explanatory visual, not fake product evidence. Do not invent claims, measurements, UI results or source citations. Do not transplant reference narration or mixed music/SFX into an edit. Distinguish listening from waveform/ASR analysis.

## Efficient delivery
Cache source hashes, transcripts, assets and edit maps; do not re-transcribe unchanged audio. Sample representative frames and uncertain transitions, then broaden only where needed. Keep logs/contact sheets local; final response is a brief file link unless the user requests previews. Preserve source, earlier outputs and a project handoff. No unsupported exact token-savings claims.
